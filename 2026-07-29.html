Significant
Jul 28, 2026 — Anthropic Research (Frontier Red Team)
Discovering cryptographic weaknesses with Claude (permalink fetched this run) reports two genuinely new results produced by an internal Claude "Mythos Preview" model with light human prompting. Against HAWK — a lattice-based digital signature scheme under NIST post-quantum review that had survived ~two years of expert scrutiny — Claude found an improved attack that halves the effective keysize: HAWK-256's expected attack cost falls from 264 to 238 (~67 million× less work), found in roughly 60 hours of autonomous effort at an estimated ~$100K in API cost. Cross-cutting observations

  • "Are open weights a cyber risk?" is now the axis the whole industry is aligning on. In one 48-hour window: Amodei's position piece (open weights fine, but gate the most capable models and the chips), a ~$8T coalition signing the opposite letter with Anthropic pointedly absent, Hugging Face's forensic timeline of a real agent intrusion, Import AI's "accidental AI hacker," and — underneath it all — a frontier model that just found novel cryptographic attacks. Every one of these is an argument about the same question, and the sides are now corporate, not rhetorical.
  • AI as a working cryptographer, not a demo. Anthropic's HAWK and AES results are the first time a frontier lab has published novel cryptographic weaknesses found mostly-autonomously — against a NIST post-quantum candidate that had survived two years of human review. The careful "no practical impact today" framing is doing a lot of work: the news isn't that AES fell (it didn't), it's that the tool that could eventually threaten it is now in the loop, at ~$100K/result.
  • Protocol velocity: MCP shipped a spec and the ecosystem absorbed it in a day. The 2026-07-28 stateless-core spec dropped and, within 48 hours, Claude shipped product support, AWS AgentCore made it a one-call upgrade, and Willison wrote the practitioner setup guide. Compare this to how long HTTP or OAuth revisions take to propagate — the agent-infrastructure layer is now iterating at model-release cadence.
  • Opus 5's real story is deliberate capability withholding. Zvi's two write-ups converge on a subtle point: Opus 5 matches Fable-class general capability at half the price, but Anthropic chose to leave out the cyber/bio "juice." That's the open-weights debate rendered as a product decision — a closed lab can ship a strong model while sandbagging the exact capabilities the policy fight is about. You can't do that with weights you've published.
  • The majors talk about their own engineering now. Pragmatic Engineer's "Inside Anthropic" (a year-long migration compressed to 11 days), Latent Space on OpenAI's ChatGPT Work harness, and O'Reilly on teaching coding after AI all point the same way: the interesting artifact is increasingly the harness and the workflow, not the model. The labs are becoming case studies in how AI-native software teams actually operate.

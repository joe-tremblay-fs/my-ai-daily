A consultancy published a working sleeper agent, the recipe, and the price: one node, one day. TNG Technology Consulting trained a backdoored Qwen 27B that stays dormant until triggered, exfiltrates data on activation, denies having hidden behaviour under direct interrogation, and retains 96.5–100% of baseline benchmark performance — so nothing in an ordinary eval suite flags it. Training cost: about one day on one 8×B200 node. Defenses that worked assume detection fails: sandboxing (OMAC) and a real-time guardrail model. Published Aug 13; missed by the previous two briefs. huggingface.co/blog
DeepSeek's price change took effect today — and it's congestion pricing, not a price rise. V4-Pro goes from a flat $0.435 / $0.87 per million to $0.66 / $1.98 off-peak and $1.32 / $3.96 peak; V4-Flash from $0.14 / $0.28 to $0.22 / $0.66 off-peak. The detail nobody led with: 17 of every 24 hours stay at the half-price off-peak rate. The stated reason — "to allocate resources more reasonably" — is a capacity admission, and the clearest signal yet that the binding constraint in Chinese frontier AI is GPUs, not capability. Tim O'Reilly's Apache essay resolved to a permalink, and it isn't about open weights at all. The Apache Lesson for AI argues the thing that mattered about open source was architectural composability — Apache beat Netscape and Microsoft by staying small with a clean extension layer, because modularity, not features, was the moat. His charge: the labs are baking personality and guardrails into the weights until models become "appliances you rent." Bob Young: "What we really sell to our customers is control." The cheapest answer to Anthropic's saturated-benchmark problem is sitting at 3 karma. Second Look Research proposed on LessWrong that every frontier release should trigger a re-run of the existing safety papers — CoT monitorability, filler tokens, subliminal learning — at $0 to $5,000 per experiment, publishing nulls and keeping a tracker. "Adding a new model is a single terminal command away." A saturated benchmark is uninformative as a threshold but still informative as a series; nobody generates the series, because null results don't publish. lesswrong.com
